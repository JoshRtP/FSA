# Official-Document-Based Plan to Achieve FSA Silver or Gold Through Benchmarking or Direct Verification

**Prepared:** 12 June 2026
**Purpose:** A decision-ready plan for achieving **Farm Sustainability Assessment (FSA) Silver or above** either by benchmarking an existing sustainability scheme or by directly verifying a stand-alone farm or Farm Management Group (FMG).
**Status:** Working implementation plan based on SAI Platform's current public documents; it is not an approval, legal opinion, or document issued by SAI Platform.

---

## 1. Executive decision and target outcome

### Recommended outcome by route

- **Benchmarking route:** pursue **FSA Benchmark Equivalent — Silver**, while designing the scheme and study to close all feasible gaps toward Gold. “Equivalent” is the correct benchmarking target when the scheme has both qualifying content/governance and an assurance method meeting FSA minimum verification requirements. “Aligned” is weaker: it covers content and governance but lacks an assurance method and therefore does not, by itself, support an external benchmark performance claim.[R4, pp. 5–7]

The final intended outcome should be documented as:

> **[Scheme name and version] is listed by SAI Platform in the FSA Benchmarking Results as FSA 3.0 Benchmark Equivalent at Silver [or Gold] level for the approved crop, geography, producer, and assurance scope.**

- **Direct-verification route:** pursue a registered Letter of Attestation showing the stand-alone farm's verified Silver/Gold level or the FMG's verified percentage claim by level. Build a scoring buffer before formal sampling because audit-stage corrective action cannot be used to improve the verified result.[R8, pp. 27–33; R9, pp. 20–24]

Do **not** describe this result as “SAI Silver certification.” FSA is not a certification standard, and SAI Platform is the organization that owns and operates FSA.[R2, “Is the FSA a certification standard?”; R4, pp. 3 and 12]

### Critical distinction: benchmarking is not direct FSA verification

There are two different routes that are often confused:

1. **Scheme benchmarking:** compares an existing sustainability scheme against FSA 3.0 and can result in Benchmark **Equivalent** or **Aligned** status at a performance level.
2. **Direct FSA verification:** farms complete the FSA Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ), an approved Verification Body audits the FSA management system and a sample of farms, and the Verification Body issues a **Letter of Attestation** stating the verified performance level.[R2, “What are my options…” and “How does FSA Verification work?”]

Use **Part I (Sections 2–10)** if the organization owns or controls an existing sustainability standard/program and wants that scheme benchmarked. Use **Part II (Sections 11–23)** if the goal is to obtain a verified claim for one farm or an FMG. Part II is a complete operational route, not merely an appendix. Do not commission a benchmarking study when the desired result is a direct farm/FMG Letter of Attestation.

### What benchmarking success requires

A defensible benchmark Silver-or-above outcome requires all of the following:

- the scheme’s normative requirements adequately cover enough of the FSA 3.0 SAQ at the target level;
- the scheme meets the applicable governance requirements;
- for **Equivalent** status, the scheme’s assurance/verification approach meets FSA minimum verification requirements;
- SAI Platform accepts and publishes the benchmark result in its FSA Benchmarking Results;
- any later benchmark claim is supported by third-party assessment under the equivalent scheme, mass-balance records, acceptable claim language, and SAI Platform review of the draft claim.[R4, pp. 6–8]

## 3. Define the benchmark scope before hiring the consultant

A narrow, accurate scope is easier to evidence and less likely to create misleading claims. Approve a one-page **Benchmark Scope Charter** containing:

1. **Scheme identity:** legal owner, public name, exact standard/program name, version, effective date, and status of earlier versions.
2. **Normative document universe:** every mandatory standard, annex, interpretation, policy, assurance rule, sanction rule, auditor rule, and claims rule proposed as benchmark evidence.
3. **Eligible users:** individual farms, farm groups, buyers, processors, or other actors.
4. **Agricultural scope:** cultivated crops/products covered; identify exclusions. FSA is intended for cultivated agricultural crops, not every agricultural or wild-harvest system.[R2, “Does the FSA apply…”]
5. **Geographic scope:** countries/regions and any country-specific legal or add-on requirements relied upon.
6. **Assurance scope:** audit frequency, sampling, audit type, auditor competence, oversight, nonconformity closure, certificate/attestation, and traceability controls.
7. **Performance model:** whether every certified/assessed participant meets one common level or whether the scheme has tiers.
8. **Target:** Benchmark Equivalent Silver as the minimum acceptance criterion; Gold gap analysis as a required study output.
9. **Intended claim:** exact business-to-business claim and supply-chain use case.

### Go/no-go eligibility screen

Before commissioning the full study, confirm:

- there is a functioning scheme—not merely a corporate policy or aspirational guidance;
- requirements are mandatory, auditable, version-controlled, and accessible to participants/auditors;
- the scheme owner can authorize changes needed to close gaps;
- the assurance model can support **Equivalent**, not only Aligned, status;
- the proposed scope is genuinely implemented and not broader than the evidence;
- the organization can maintain the scheme and re-benchmark when required.

If assurance is absent or immature, either (a) budget and implement assurance upgrades before final submission, or (b) accept that the initial result may only be **Benchmark Aligned**.[R4, pp. 5–6]

## 4. How to identify and appoint a benchmarking consultant

### Mandatory selection rule

Use a consultant that **SAI Platform confirms is currently approved to conduct FSA benchmarking** for the intended project. Do not assume that an FSA Verification Body is also an approved Benchmarking Consultant; benchmarking and verification are separate functions and document frameworks.[R5, p. 7]

The public Resource Centre does not presently expose a definitive consultant roster or a public consultant-application form. Therefore, the authoritative selection step is to request the current approved list from the FSA Help Desk and have SAI confirm the selected firm’s status in writing.

### Consultant procurement process

**Step 1 — Longlist.** Ask SAI Platform for at least three approved consultants. Supplement the list only with firms that can provide written evidence of current SAI approval.

**Step 2 — Issue an RFP.** Require bidders to price two options:

- **Option A — owner-prepared / consultant-validated:** your team creates the complete first-pass benchmark crosswalk; the consultant reviews, challenges, corrects, and submits/validates it.
- **Option B — consultant-led full benchmark:** consultant creates the crosswalk and study in collaboration with your subject-matter experts.

Both models are used in the market, but SAI must confirm the selected workflow complies with the current protocol.

**Step 3 — Score proposals.** Use the following weighted scorecard:

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence required |
|---|---:|---|
| Current SAI approval and protocol competence | 20% | SAI confirmation; lead consultant name; recent training |
| FSA 3.0 benchmark experience | 20% | At least three relevant completed studies; outcomes and references |
| Crop/geography/scheme-model expertise | 15% | Comparable standards, languages, and legal contexts |
| Assurance/governance expertise | 15% | Experience designing and assessing audit, oversight, sanctions, and complaints systems |
| Method and quality control | 15% | Crosswalk approach, evidence rules, independent technical review, change control |
| Ability to support gap closure without compromising independence | 5% | Clear separation of advisory and final assessment judgments |
| Schedule, availability, and responsiveness | 5% | Named team and milestone dates |
| Transparent fixed/estimated fees | 5% | Assumptions, exclusions, SAI fees, travel, and revision rounds |

**Step 4 — Interview finalists.** Ask each finalist to explain:

- how partial coverage and conditional requirements are treated;
- how legal requirements can be relied upon across the approved geography;
- how they test that a scheme requirement is mandatory and assured in practice;
- what creates Equivalent versus Aligned status;
- how conflicts of interest are managed if they also advise on scheme revisions;
- which forms are filed by the consultant, which by the scheme owner, and which are generated by SAI;
- who communicates with SAI and who owns the final workpapers.

**Step 5 — Contract for deliverables, not merely hours.** At minimum require: confirmed scope; controlled-template register; preliminary score; issue/gap log; complete content crosswalk; governance assessment; assurance assessment; Silver and Gold gap-closure recommendations; final submission package; response-to-comments log; final approved benchmark report/result; and an owner handover file.

### Red flags

Reject or escalate a bidder that promises Silver/Gold before reviewing documents, cannot prove current approval, treats guidance as mandatory evidence, ignores assurance/governance, proposes using an obsolete SAQ/protocol, will not provide the completed crosswalk, or suggests public claims before SAI publication/approval.

Those controlled forms must be obtained from SAI Platform at project launch. Do not invent, rename, or independently recreate a form for filing.

| **Governance assessment questionnaire/checklist** *(exact controlled title to be confirmed)* | Scheme owner; assessed by consultant | Yes | Yes | Demonstrates credible ownership, document control, stakeholder/input mechanisms, complaints, integrity, and other current protocol requirements. Benchmark claims require content and governance.[R4, pp. 5–6] |
| **Assurance/verification assessment questionnaire/checklist** *(exact controlled title to be confirmed)* | Scheme owner; assessed by consultant | Yes for Equivalent | Yes | Demonstrates that the scheme meets FSA minimum verification requirements. Without an assurance method, the result is alignment rather than equivalence.[R4, pp. 5–6] |
| **FSA 3.0 SAQ** | Farmers under direct FSA implementation; benchmark consultant uses it as reference | Reference for benchmark; not normally completed by every scheme farm merely to benchmark the scheme | Confirm | It is the farm-level 109-question yes/no instrument for direct self-assessment and the content reference for benchmarking.[R3, p. 32] |
| **Benchmark claim eligibility checklist** | Scheme/user intending to claim | After approval, before claim | Submit draft claim to SAI | Checks third-party assessment under an equivalent scheme, mass balance, claim type/language, and SAI review.[R4, pp. 6–8] |
| **Draft claim/communications approval request** | Claiming organization | If making a claim | Yes | SAI guidance states draft claims are submitted to `fsatool@saiplatform.org` for review.[R4, pp. 7–8] |
| **Continuous Improvement Plan** | Direct-implementation FMG / stand-alone farm as applicable | Not a substitute for benchmark study | Audited in direct route | Required in direct FSA group implementation; must be time-bound with targets and responsibilities.[R2, “How does FSA enable…”] |

### Minimum evidence packet accompanying the questionnaires

Build a numbered, read-only evidence library and cite each file, version, page, and clause in the crosswalk. Include at least:

- scheme standard and all mandatory annexes/add-ons;
- definitions and scope rules;
- governance charter, ownership, decision rights, and document-change procedure;
- stakeholder consultation and revision records;
- complaints, appeals, impartiality, conflicts, and grievance procedures;
- assurance manual, audit protocol/checklists, sampling rules, audit-frequency rules, remote-audit rules, nonconformity grading and closure, sanctions, suspension/withdrawal, and certificate/attestation templates;
- auditor/assurance-body competence, approval, training, monitoring, and oversight rules;
- traceability/mass-balance requirements and records;
- participant registry and evidence the scheme is operational;
- claims/communications rules;
- applicable legislation matrix if law is relied upon to close content gaps;
- translations and translation-control statement where relevant.

### Crosswalk quality rule

For every FSA requirement claimed as covered, the study should show:

1. exact FSA question/control reference;
2. exact scheme document, version, clause, and quoted or summarized obligation;
3. why the obligation fully covers the FSA intent and scope;
4. whether it applies to every participant in the proposed benchmark scope;
5. how an auditor tests it and what objective evidence is expected;
6. any dependency on geography, crop, producer type, tier, legal requirement, or scheme combination;
7. consultant judgment and unresolved issue status.

Do not count optional guidance, examples, recommendations, or unenforced commitments as mandatory coverage.

## 6. Silver/Gold gap analysis and remediation method

### Phase 1 — Preliminary desktop benchmark

The consultant performs an initial crosswalk and issues a traffic-light gap register:

- **Green:** fully covered, mandatory, in scope, and auditable;
- **Amber:** partial, ambiguous, conditional, jurisdiction-specific, or weakly assured;
- **Red:** absent or contradicted;
- **Out of scope:** only where the protocol permits and rationale is documented.

The consultant must calculate the preliminary result using the current SAI-controlled scoring logic. Do not rely on thresholds copied from obsolete FSA versions. The official public materials confirm that the benchmarked performance level is determined by comparing scheme content with FSA 3.0, but the current protocol/template supplied by SAI must control the calculation.[R4, p. 5]

### Phase 2 — Close Silver blockers first

Prioritize remediation in this order:

1. **Essential/FSA-critical content gaps** and any automatic blockers identified by the current protocol;
2. governance gaps that prevent any benchmark claim;
3. assurance gaps that would downgrade the outcome from Equivalent to Aligned;
4. content gaps necessary to achieve Silver;
5. low-effort changes that build margin above the Silver boundary;
6. Gold gaps, grouped into feasible-now and future-roadmap items.

For every standard revision, complete governance approval, version control, effective-date planning, participant/auditor communication, training, and assurance-tool updates. A paper change that has not entered the scheme’s controlled system may not be accepted as evidence.

### Phase 3 — Internal readiness review

Before final submission, the scheme owner’s accountable executive signs a readiness memo confirming:

- all documents in the benchmark are final, approved, and effective (or SAI has accepted the transition arrangement);
- the approved scope is accurate;
- every crosswalk citation has been checked;
- assurance controls are operational and supported by records;
- no known gap is concealed;
- the expected result is at least Equivalent Silver with an agreed safety margin;
- claim language has not been used prematurely.

## 7. Submission, SAI review, and decision workflow

Because the current public Resource Centre does not disclose all filing mechanics, the consultant and scheme owner must agree the exact workflow with SAI in writing. Use the following controlled sequence:

1. **Initiation:** send the scope charter to the FSA Help Desk; obtain current forms, approved-consultant confirmation, process, fees, and named SAI contact.
2. **Registration/application:** complete and file SAI’s current intake/application form and any agreement/fee acceptance.
3. **Consultant appointment:** provide appointment confirmation and conflict-of-interest disclosure if requested.
4. **Preliminary benchmark:** complete draft content, governance, and assurance assessments; receive preliminary level and gap report.
5. **Scheme remediation:** approve and implement revisions; update all evidence and crosswalks.
6. **Quality review:** consultant performs independent technical review and signs off the complete filing package.
7. **Formal submission:** consultant/owner submits the controlled forms, study, evidence index, scope statement, and declarations through SAI’s required channel.
8. **SAI review:** maintain a response-to-comments log showing question, owner, response, evidence, decision, and closure date. Do not make untracked substitutions to the evidence set.
9. **Decision/publication:** obtain written decision and verify that the scheme name, version, level, Equivalent/Aligned status, scope, combinations, and limitations are correctly recorded in the **FSA Benchmarking Results**.
10. **Claims approval:** separately run the benchmark claim checklist and send any draft claim to SAI for approval before publication.[R4, pp. 6–8]
11. **Handover/maintenance:** archive final workpapers and create change triggers for re-benchmarking.

## 9. Post-approval claims, operations, and maintenance

### Claim controls

A benchmark result alone does not allow every product or volume to be described at the benchmark level. The official benchmark-claim checklist asks whether the product volume was third-party assessed against a system with FSA Bronze/Silver/Gold **equivalence** status, whether an appropriate mass-balance system tracks incoming/outgoing volumes, whether the claim language is compliant, and whether SAI checked the draft claim.[R4, pp. 6–8]

Use these controls:

- permit claims only for the published scheme version and approved scope;
- retain certificates/attestations showing the relevant producers were third-party assessed under the equivalent scheme;
- maintain mass-balance records for claimed volumes;
- state clearly that the result concerns **FSA**, not “SAI Silver”;
- distinguish a Benchmark Claim from an FSA Verified Claim;
- do not use the FSA name, performance level, or logo as an on-pack FSA claim; online claims are possible subject to guidance and approval.[R4, pp. 12–15]

### Maintenance triggers

Create a benchmark-impact review whenever any of the following changes:

- scheme standard, annex, interpretation, tier, crop, geography, or participant scope;
- assurance method, audit frequency, sampling, auditor competence, oversight, sanctions, or claims rules;
- applicable legislation materially relied upon in the crosswalk;
- FSA SAQ, Benchmarking Protocol, governance/assurance criteria, or claims guidance;
- ownership/governance of the scheme;
- published benchmark validity or re-benchmark deadline.

At least quarterly, verify the scheme’s entry in the current FSA Benchmarking Results and retain a dated copy. SAI has previously required schemes to re-benchmark against a new FSA version, so continued status must not be assumed.[R7]

## Part II — Detailed operational plan for direct FSA verification

Use this route when the goal is to verify the performance of a **stand-alone farm** or a **Farm Management Group (FMG)** directly against the FSA rather than to obtain equivalence for a separate sustainability scheme. Direct verification produces a verified FSA Performance Level Claim and a registered **Letter of Attestation (LoA)**. It is not certification.[R2; R8, pp. 4–8; R9, pp. 4–8]

### 11. Choose the direct-verification operating model

Make this decision before completing SAQs or contracting the Verification Body (VB).

| Decision | Stand-alone farm | Farm Management Group |
|---|---|---|
| Best fit | One farm/business wants its own verified result | A buyer, cooperative, processor, or other coordinator organizes multiple supplying farms |
| What is verified | The farm's FSA Management System and its complete Audit Self-Assessment | The FMG's FSA Management System plus a random sample of farm Audit Self-Assessments and a second random sample of on-farm audits |
| Continuous Improvement Plan | Optional; auditor notes it but is not required to verify it against FSA requirements | Required and audited as part of the FSA Management System |
| Sampling efficiency | None; the farm is audited | Sample results generate the claim for the FMG, creating efficiency but also group-wide exposure to deviations |
| Claim owner | The farm owns the LoA even if a buyer contracts the VB | The FMG Coordinator owns/manages the group claim and LoA |
| Management-system audit duration | Expected to be about half a day, depending on complexity and readiness | Expected to be one day and no more than two days, depending on complexity and readiness |
| On-farm audit duration | Generally half to one day | Generally half to one day for each selected farm |

The stand-alone route simplifies management-system requirements but loses the sampling efficiencies of an FMG.[R2; R9, pp. 10–13] For an FMG, recognize the central risk: because sampled results generate the performance claim for the whole group, inaccurate farmer answers and deviations can affect the claimed percentages across the entire Audit Self-Assessment group.[R8, pp. 11–13 and 27–31]

### 12. Set the target and verification scope

Approve a **Direct Verification Scope Memo** before implementation. It should state:

- legal name and address of the stand-alone farm or FMG Coordinator;
- FMG name and complete farm population, if applicable;
- crop or crops in scope and relevant growing/harvest periods;
- country and region; obtain a special agreement from SAI Platform before relying on a multi-country or greater-than-5,000-farm FMG arrangement referenced by the audit guidance;
- farm-gate boundary and any farm-controlled transport included in scope;
- target verified result: preferably Silver, with a readiness buffer above the minimum;
- intended first claim period, whether the previous harvest must be covered, and intended customers;
- management-system owner, volume-accounting owner, farmer-engagement lead, and evidence/document controller;
- target audit and LoA dates.

For FSA 3.0, the operational readiness thresholds are:

| Level | Essential questions | Intermediate questions | Advanced questions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bronze | 100% | 50% | No minimum |
| **Silver** | **100%** | **75%** | **50%** |
| Gold | 100% | 100% | 75% |

Treat these thresholds as the minimum, not the project target. For Silver readiness, aim for 100% Essential, at least 85% Intermediate, and at least 60% Advanced in internal assessments so that a small number of verified downward deviations do not cause a level change. Confirm the scoring generated by the current official SAQ file before contracting or claiming a result.[R10]

### 13. Obtain and control the direct-verification document set

At project launch, download current versions from SAI Platform's FSA Resource Centre and obtain any VB-controlled forms. Record the file name, version/date, source URL, download date, language, and document owner in a controlled register.

**Normative and operating documents to control:**

1. FSA 3.0 Implementation Framework;
2. current FSA 3.0 Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ);
3. Audit Control Points FSA 3.0 SAQ;
4. Audit Control Points FSA Management System;
5. Guidance for Third-Party Verification of FMG FSA 3.0 Claims **or** Guidance for Third-Party Verification of Stand-Alone Farm FSA 3.0 Claims;
6. current Requirements for FSA Verification Bodies and Auditors;
7. current approved-VB list, verification fee table, claims guide, and any current verification guidance notes;
8. for FMGs, the FSA Verification Audit Annual Reporting Template;
9. Topic Guides, Farmer Handbook, Training Toolkit, Red and Amber PPP List, and other supporting tools relevant to the crop and region.[R1; R6; R11]

The Resource Centre identifies the audit guides and both Audit Control Point files as normative documents. As of 12 June 2026, it lists the VB/Auditor Requirements as updated May 2025, the Verification Integrity Oversight Programme as September 2025, and the approved-VB resource as updated April 2026.[R1; R6; R11]

### 14. Build the FSA Management System before audit

#### 14.1 FMG setup and accountability

For an FMG, create an auditable system that answers four questions: **Who is in the group? Who is accountable? How will farms improve? How are claimable volumes controlled?** The FSA Management System audit tests FMG setup, management accountability/oversight, the continuous-improvement program, and the accuracy/reliability of volume accounting.[R8, pp. 13–17]

Create and maintain:

- an approved FMG scope and organization chart;
- a complete farm master list with stable farm ID, farmer name, location, crop, area, expected/actual volume, join/leave dates, and status;
- signed farm participation agreements authorizing assessment, random selection, audits, worker interviews, information sharing, and removal for unjustified refusal;
- named FSA Management System owner and deputies;
- documented procedures for farm onboarding/removal, data validation, records retention, complaints, change management, farmer support, and internal monitoring;
- training and communication records showing farmers understand the FSA, SAQ answers, target level, evidence expectations, audit access, and continuous-improvement actions;
- controls over outsourced activities. If another organization delivers part of the system, contracts and monitoring records must demonstrate that its work meets FSA requirements.[R8, pp. 14–17]

For a stand-alone farm, document management accountability and the volume-accounting system. A Continuous Improvement Plan is optional for stand-alone verification, although maintaining one is recommended.[R9, pp. 10–13]

#### 14.2 Priority screening and continuous improvement for FMGs

Complete the FSA priority-screening process and retain the **Priority Screening Summary Report**. Use it with baseline farm information to identify the most relevant sustainability issues. Create a time-bound **Continuous Improvement Plan (CIP)** that includes, at minimum:

- priority issue and baseline condition;
- target/result and performance indicator;
- actions and milestones;
- accountable owner and participating farms;
- resources/budget and support needed;
- due dates;
- monitoring method and progress status.

Use the Implementation Framework's CIP and Progress Reporting templates or an equivalent format that preserves their required content. Keep evidence of implementation—training materials, attendance records, action plans, assessments, monitoring results, and management reviews—because the auditor checks both the plan and evidence of engagement/support.[R3, p. 32; R8, pp. 15–17]

#### 14.3 Implement volume accounting

Set up the volume-accounting system before the first claimable intake. FSA requires mass balance as a minimum and uses a **quantity-credit method**. Maintain a separate credit account for each crop at each claimed FSA performance level; deduct claimed outputs without allowing a negative balance; document conversion factors; and permit reassignment from a higher to a lower performance level only where sufficient higher-level credits exist.[R5, p. 22]

At minimum retain:

- eligible-farm list linked to intake records;
- actual incoming volumes by farm, crop, date, and verified level;
- inventory/opening balances and adjustments;
- processing yields and documented conversion factors;
- sales/output declarations and customer claim records;
- separate Bronze, Silver, and Gold credit ledgers;
- control preventing double counting or negative balances;
- reconciliation, exception review, approval, and management-review records.

Run an internal end-to-end trace test before audit: select incoming farm volumes, follow them into the credit ledger, recalculate conversion and output deductions, then trace a claimed sale back to sufficient credits. The FSA Management System auditor will sample-test inputs, outputs, conversion factors, calculations, and supporting evidence. The FSA audit does not extend to a chain-of-custody audit beyond the FMG.[R8, pp. 14–17]

### 15. Complete SAQs and prepare farms for Silver or above

#### 15.1 Baseline assessments

Have all participating farms complete a baseline SAQ early enough to permit remediation and farmer support. Although the VB later chooses the formal FMG **Audit Self-Assessment Sample**, full-population baseline assessments are the strongest operational control against sampling surprises.

For every `yes` answer, record the expected proof using the SAQ Audit Control Points. For every `n/a`, document why it is not applicable. For every `no`, identify the performance category, effect on the target level, owner, and improvement action. Do not coach a farmer to answer `yes` without a true and supportable practice.

Create a farm evidence file organized by SAQ question/topic. Typical evidence includes:

- applicable permits, licenses, land/tenure documents, and legal-compliance records;
- crop/field maps and production records;
- soil, nutrient, irrigation/water, pest-management, biodiversity, waste, and greenhouse-gas-related plans/records as applicable;
- current pesticide inventory and application records, safety data, prohibited/restricted-product checks, and operator training;
- equipment maintenance/calibration and health-and-safety records;
- employment contracts, wage/time records, age verification, worker policies, grievance records, and labour-provider controls;
- worker accommodation information where applicable;
- training attendance and competency records;
- third-party service contracts and monitoring evidence.

Evidence is not limited to documents. Auditors use triangulation of management/worker interviews, observation, facility/equipment inspection, and documentation. Small-scale farms may use verbal descriptions in place of written plans/policies where permitted, but the practice must still be credible and observable.[R8, pp. 20–25; R9, pp. 14–18]

#### 15.2 Silver readiness controls

Before asking the VB to select the formal sample:

1. independently review every Essential `yes` and every `n/a`;
2. re-score each farm and flag farms close to the Silver thresholds;
3. conduct internal mock audits on a representative set of farms, including worker interviews and physical inspection;
4. verify that off-site records, chemical stores, worker accommodation, and other relevant locations will be accessible;
5. correct real practices and systems **before** the formal audit process begins;
6. lock the farm master list and reconcile expected volumes;
7. brief farmers on truthful answers, confidentiality, worker access, and the consequences of refusal.

Corrective actions are not allowed during the verification audit merely to improve the audited score. After the formal audit finds a deviation, the farm/FMG may provide additional evidence about the condition that existed, but cannot fix it during that audit and count the fix toward the result. A new audit is required to obtain a better result after corrective action.[R8, pp. 27–30; R9, pp. 20–22]

### 16. Select and contract an approved Verification Body

Only a VB currently listed by SAI Platform may conduct direct FSA verification. Use SAI Platform's approved-VB page, which was reviewed February 2026, and confirm the selected VB's approval, geography, auditor availability, and relevant crop/language capability in writing.[R6]

Issue an RFP to at least three approved VBs requesting:

- verification model and scope: stand-alone or FMG, crops, country, number of farms, expected audit window, and previous-harvest request;
- named Principal Auditor and any Associate Auditors, with crop, geography, language, and worker-interview experience;
- confirmation that all work will use current FSA 3.0 normative documents;
- full fee breakdown, including audit days, travel, SAI fees, database/registration, investigation/follow-up, and re-audit;
- proposed sampling/audit timeline and deliverables;
- independence/conflict disclosure and confidentiality/data-sharing terms;
- process for deviations, escalation, LoA issuance, database registration, and annual FMG reporting.

Score proposals on current approval, relevant auditor competence, availability during the growing season, audit approach, farmer/worker language access, transparent cost, and reporting responsiveness. The engagement must have a clear contract covering audit activities, fees, terms, and information disclosure. A buyer may contract the VB, but the stand-alone farm still owns its LoA.[R9, pp. 8–10]

### 17. FMG sampling and formal Audit Self-Assessments

The VB—not the FMG—must make the random selections and retain the selection record. There are two samples.

#### 17.1 Audit Self-Assessment Sample

The VB randomly selects farms from the complete FMG list to complete the formal Audit Self-Assessment. Selection must be entirely random without prior stratification or risk assessment. A sampled SAQ completed within the previous 12 months may be submitted, but should be reviewed for currency.[R8, pp. 11–13]

| Total farms in FMG | Farms completing Audit Self-Assessment |
|---:|---:|
| 0–10 | 6 |
| 11–20 | 10 |
| 21–30 | 13 |
| 31–50 | 18 |
| 51–100 | 24 |
| 101–200 | 30 |
| 201–500 | 36 |
| 501–5,000 | 40 |

If a sampled farm refuses the SAQ without force majeure, remove that farm and its associated volume from the FMG and have another randomly selected farm complete the SAQ. The VB may select backups.[R8, pp. 12–13]

#### 17.2 On-farm audit sample

After reviewing the completed Audit Self-Assessments, the auditor makes a second entirely random selection from those farms for physical audit.[R8, pp. 17–20]

| Completed Audit Self-Assessments | Minimum on-farm audits |
|---:|---:|
| 6 | 4 |
| 10 | 4 |
| 13 | 7 |
| 18 | 7 |
| 24 | 9 |
| 30 | 9 |
| 36 | 9 |
| 40 | 9 |

A farm that refuses physical audit without force majeure and its associated volume must be removed; another randomly selected farm completes an SAQ and is audited. The VB may select one or two backups. On-farm audits must commence within two weeks of selecting the on-farm sample and within three months of selecting the Audit Self-Assessment Sample.[R8, pp. 18–20]

### 18. Execute the direct-verification audit

#### 18.1 Pre-audit work and audit plan

The VB completes and shares a documented risk assessment covering all FSA topics and the country/region, crops, and farming methods. For an FMG, it also uses the Priority Screening Summary Report. Agree a detailed audit plan, preferably placing farm audits during the growing season so relevant practices can be observed.[R8, pp. 9–11; R9, pp. 8–10]

Provide the VB with a controlled pre-audit pack:

- scope memo and contacts;
- farm master list and agreements;
- formal sampled SAQs and supporting evidence;
- Priority Screening Summary Report and CIP/progress evidence for an FMG;
- FSA Management System procedures and records;
- volume-accounting procedure, ledgers, reconciliations, conversion factors, and source records;
- prior audit/LoA and annual reports, if applicable;
- request and evidence for previous-harvest coverage, if desired.

#### 18.2 FSA Management System audit

The auditor interviews the people responsible for the management system, volume accounting, and farmer engagement/continuous improvement; reviews procedures and implementation records; and tests volume data/calculations. For an FMG, the audit normally takes one day and no more than two; for a stand-alone farm, it is expected to take about half a day.[R8, pp. 13–17; R9, pp. 10–13]

The auditor must issue the FSA Management System Audit Report within two weeks. If the system does not meet requirements, on-farm audits cannot proceed until corrections are evidenced and the auditor issues an updated report confirming the system meets requirements. The management-system verification statement is valid for three years.[R8, pp. 16–18; R9, pp. 12–13]

#### 18.3 On-farm audit day

Prepare each selected farm for this sequence:

1. **Opening meeting:** confirm participants, purpose, scope, process, confidentiality, documents, and permission for worker interviews.
2. **Management interview:** explain policies, practices, management systems, and farmer/FMG support behind SAQ answers.
3. **Document review:** present records supporting implementation.
4. **Field/facility inspection:** provide access to fields, stores, equipment, safety controls, worker accommodation, biodiversity measures, and relevant off-site locations.
5. **Confidential worker interviews:** the auditor chooses interviewees and may include family, permanent, temporary, migrant, male/female, agrochemical-handling, labour-agency workers, and the worker representative. Interviews are individual and confidential.
6. **Closing meeting:** hear deviations, respond, and provide any immediately available additional evidence.

For high-risk labour/human-rights locations, plan capacity for the audit guide's worker-interview schedule; the auditor may reduce it in lower-risk locations using the documented risk assessment:

| Workers at location | Worker interviews |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3–7 | 3 |
| 8–48 | 4 |
| 49–54 | 5 |
| 55–64 | 6 |
| Each additional 10 | Add 1, up to 25 total |

The audit scope must match the SAQ and generally ends at the farm gate, except relevant farm-controlled transport. Every SAQ question must ultimately be verified at farm level; FMG-level evidence can support answers but cannot replace confirming farm awareness and implementation.[R8, pp. 20–26; R9, pp. 13–19]

### 19. Resolve audit findings and confirm the claim

The auditor finalizes the on-farm report within two weeks of the final farm audit and holds an Audit Review Meeting with the farmer/FMG Coordinator within the same two-week period. Minute the meeting.[R8, pp. 25–27]

A **deviation** is a verified change to an SAQ answer: downward from `yes`/`n/a` to `no`, or upward from `no`/`n/a` to `yes`. Choose one documented path:

- **Path A — no deviations:** agree the assessed scores.
- **Path B — accept deviations:** accept amended verified scores without further investigation. In an FMG, accepted downward deviations may be applied across the Audit Self-Assessment group; generic upward adjustments are not allowed.
- **Path C — investigate:** provide evidence that the deviation did or did not occur in relevant sampled farms. Hold a Verification Closing Meeting within six weeks of the Audit Review Meeting to confirm the final result.

For an FMG, the claim percentages are calculated from the **number of farms** at each verified level in the random Audit Self-Assessment group, then applied to volume; they are not weighted by each farm's volume. The FMG may elect to claim a lower level, but the elected claim cannot be changed once stated on the LoA.[R8, pp. 27–31]

If the farmer/FMG and VB cannot agree, escalate through `fsatool@saiplatform.org` under the audit guide's adjudication process.[R8, pp. 33–34; R9, p. 24]

### 20. Receive, validate, and register the Letter of Attestation

The VB must issue the LoA within four weeks after completion of verification—the Audit Review Meeting date or, if Path C is used, the Verification Closing Meeting date. Check it immediately for:

- correct farm/FMGC name and address;
- FMG and country details where applicable;
- unique identification code;
- verified FSA Performance Level Claim or FMG percentage claim;
- crops in scope;
- claim start/end dates;
- Principal Auditor and issuing VB details/signature;
- any approved previous-harvest coverage.[R8, pp. 31–33; R9, pp. 22–24]

The maximum LoA validity is three years. The VB must register it in the FSA Verification Database within 12 weeks of verification completion, and **the LoA is not valid until registered**. Obtain confirmation of registration and retain the registered LoA/unique code.[R8, pp. 31–34; R9, pp. 22–24]

### 22. Operate and maintain verification over the three-year cycle

#### FMG annual controls

Each year, send the VB the required annual report and complete farm list. An FMG LoA is invalidated by turnover greater than 10% in membership or an increase greater than 10% in total farm count; after crossing a threshold, the LoA remains valid for six months to allow re-audit. A new sampled verification is required to continue external claims, although the management-system audit need not be repeated if still within its three-year validity.[R8, pp. 31–34; R11]

At least quarterly:

- reconcile actual incoming and outgoing volumes and claim credits;
- confirm no credit account is negative and no volume is double-counted;
- update the farm list and calculate membership turnover/growth;
- monitor CIP progress and retain implementation evidence;
- review new/changed legal requirements and relevant PPP lists;
- train new farms and staff;
- investigate complaints, suspected inaccurate SAQs, and claim errors;
- verify customer-facing FSA statements remain within approved guidance.

#### Re-verification calendar

Start re-verification planning at least six months before LoA expiry. Reconfirm current FSA documents and VB approval; refresh all farm data and baseline assessments; close real practice gaps before formal selection; schedule during the crop season; and ensure no claim continues beyond registered LoA validity.

#### Operational readiness gate before formal VB sampling

Do not authorize formal sampling until every item below is `yes`:

- [ ] Scope, farm population, crop, and claim period are final and reconcilable.
- [ ] Current normative documents and scoring file are controlled.
- [ ] The FSA Management System has passed an internal control-point review.
- [ ] FMG Priority Screening Summary Report and CIP are approved and operational.
- [ ] Volume accounting passed an end-to-end trace and no ledger is negative.
- [ ] All farms completed baseline SAQs; all Essential answers and `n/a` responses were independently reviewed.
- [ ] Every farm meets Silver internally with an agreed score buffer, or management has accepted the statistical claim risk.
- [ ] Farms know they may be selected, permit access/interviews, and have current evidence ready.
- [ ] Practice corrections are complete before formal audit; there is no plan to rely on audit-stage corrective action.
- [ ] Approved VB, audit window, costs, reporting dates, and previous-harvest request are agreed.
